Climate rationale development and review

For a concept note or funding proposal in water supply and sanitation to the Green Climate Fund (GCF) or a similar fund. I develop the climate rationale with your proposal team, or I review the rationale or proposal you have drafted before you submit it.

Water supply and sanitation

I work on water supply and sanitation alike. Sanitation components are often presented with a weaker climate case than water supply, although the links are direct: floods damage latrines and treatment works and spread faecal contamination, pits overflow in heavy rain, drought undermines water-based sanitation, and untreated wastewater emits methane. A strong rationale makes these links explicit and supports them with data.

What the Fund assesses

The Fund assesses each proposal on its climate impact potential. Common gaps in water and sanitation proposals include:

  • hazards described but not linked to activities;
  • development deficits presented as climate impacts;
  • sanitation components with no link to climate hazards;
  • climate data without a source or at the wrong scale;
  • vulnerability described in words where data would allow numbers.

Developing the climate rationale

I work with your proposal team and draft the climate rationale: the chain from observed and projected climate hazards to the proposed activities, with a source for every figure.

What you receive

  • The climate rationale section, drafted and ready to insert in the concept note or funding proposal
  • A technical annex with the climate data, projections and methods at the scale of the project
  • The climate-attributable increment separated from the development baseline
  • Vulnerability and exposure quantified where data allow
  • A source log for every figure
  • A list of remaining data gaps, each with the action needed to close it

How it works

  1. We agree the scope, the target fund and the timeline.
  2. I collect and analyse the climate data and projections, and check what national policy documents already say.
  3. I draft the section and annex, and revise them with your team.
  4. You submit. Work is done from Berlin unless field work is agreed at the outset.
  5. On request, I help answer the Fund's review comments after submission.

Reviewing your rationale before submission

An assessment of an existing climate rationale or proposal before it is submitted, while there is still time to correct it. The method is the same in every review. What varies is the standard the document is measured against, agreed at the start and stated in the memorandum.

Own standardDoes the document deliver what it sets out to do: claims supported, logic consistent, figures and annexes in agreement?
National policyIs it aligned with the Nationally Determined Contribution, the National Adaptation Plan and the sector strategy?
Fund submissionWould it meet a fund's investment criteria: evidence chain, climate additionality, data provenance?

What you receive

  • Every data claim traced to its source
  • Narrative, figures and annexes checked against each other
  • A memorandum separating required corrections from recommended ones
  • A debrief with the drafting team, and corrections drafted on request

Questions a review answers

  • Is the document ready for a fund, or a sector document in climate language?
  • How much of the problem is a development deficit, and how much is attributable to climate change?
  • Does the water balance hold under the projected climate?
  • Are the analyses the argument relies on actually in the document?
  • Which gaps can be closed at the desk, and which need field work?

Independence. I do not review a document I have drafted or corrected. Any later review of such a document is carried out by someone else.

No review can guarantee a fund's approval. A review identifies what technical appraisal is likely to challenge, while there is still time to correct it.
