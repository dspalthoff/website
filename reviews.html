Climate finance

Proposal quality assurance

An assessment of an existing climate rationale or proposal before it is submitted, while there is still time to correct it.

The standard is yours to choose

The method is the same in every review. What varies is the standard the document is measured against, agreed at the start and stated in the memorandum.

Own standardDoes the document deliver what it sets out to do: claims supported, logic consistent, figures and annexes in agreement?
National policyIs it aligned with the Nationally Determined Contribution, the National Adaptation Plan and the sector strategy?
Fund submissionWould it meet a fund's investment criteria: evidence chain, climate additionality, data provenance?

What you receive

  • Every data claim traced to its source
  • Narrative, figures and annexes checked against each other
  • A memorandum separating required corrections from recommended ones
  • A debrief with the drafting team, and corrections drafted on request

Questions a review answers

  • Is the document ready for a fund, or a sector document in climate language?
  • How much of the problem is a development deficit, and how much is attributable to climate change?
  • Does the water balance hold under the projected climate?
  • Are the analyses the argument relies on actually in the document?
  • Which gaps can be closed at the desk, and which need field work?

Independence. I do not review a document I have drafted or corrected. Any later review of such a document is carried out by someone else.

No review can guarantee a fund's approval. A review identifies what technical appraisal is likely to challenge, while there is still time to correct it.
